## Honours Thesis

### Collective purchasing under private information: Efficiency and cost recovery

**Abstract**

We study collective purchasing in which a given group of users with privately known productivities bids through a mediator against outside bidders in a second-price auction for a fixed divisible resource sold as a single lot. Sharing can improve resource use and make additional purchases worthwhile, but private information may prevent efficient purchasing from recovering expected costs. Under a regularity condition, we characterise the surplus-maximising mechanism subject to truthful reporting, voluntary participation, and expected cost recovery. It uses adjusted productivity weights to choose the group’s bid and internal allocation. When efficient purchasing cannot recover expected costs, the mechanism restores cost recovery with the smallest possible loss of expected surplus. Its bid is no higher than the efficient benchmark, so beneficial purchases may be forgone. We also identify conditions under which acquired capacity is weakly more concentrated among higher-productivity users, potentially reducing total use value. With independent productivity draws and fixed total capacity, Vickrey–Clarke–Groves payments implement the exact first best and recover expected costs for all sufficiently large finite groups. As group size tends to infinity, we also characterise the division of expected surplus between the mediator and users, relating their shares to the production function.
